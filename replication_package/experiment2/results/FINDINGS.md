## Headline

**A realised output does not establish a preference.** On states where the model prefers the
truth and the deployment sampler leaves the false answer reachable, misleading outputs occur at the
rate the recorded distribution predicts — 0.196 against 0.197 (S1). Whether the effect can be seen
at all is set by the decoding rule: 123 of 480 Gemma states and 31 of 480 Qwen states are eligible,
and in 288 / 384 the sampler makes the false answer unreachable (S2).

**A falsehood no preference produced** (S4): an external random selector emitted the false report
on 0.531 of trials while the model's own scores favoured the truth in 411 / 480 and 415 / 480 of the
same states. Any outcome-defined method labels that selector deceptive.

States that *prefer* the falsehood are accuracy failures, not selection misattribution, and are
counted separately (S3); a standing 0.372 bias toward `Yes` means polarities are never pooled (S5).

## S1 — A truth-preferring state produces misleading outputs at a predictable rate

The §4.4 claim, demonstrated. Among states where the sampler leaves both answers reachable
**and** the truthful one is preferred:

| | eligible | misleading draws | predicted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gemma, margin 2 affirm | 34/60 | **0.1963** | 0.1972 |
| Gemma, margin 3 affirm | 30/60 | 0.1542 | 0.1550 |
| Gemma, margin 1 deny | 29/60 | 0.1505 | 0.1471 |
| Qwen, margin 2 affirm | 19/60 | **0.2511** | 0.2605 |
| Qwen, margin 3 affirm | 6/60 | 0.3200 | 0.3300 |

**About one rollout in five is misleading, from a policy that prefers the truth**, and the
rate matches what the recorded distribution predicts to within 0.01 in every cell. A single
sampled trajectory therefore carries no information about the preference it was drawn from —
which is the whole of §2.3, now with a known truth rather than a constraint satisfaction.

**For the paper:** this is the §4.4 result. It supersedes nothing in F8, which found the same
shape in Experiment 1's guessing game (14/40 states, 20.5% of draws); what is new here is that
the falsehood is false **relative to the log written into the state**, verified by a
manipulation check that passes 80/80 on both models.

**The reported quantity is the draw fraction, not "did any draw realise it".** At R = 200 the
indicator saturates to 1.000 in every eligible cell, so it measures the draw count rather
than the model. It is shown alongside, and should not be quoted.

## S2 — Whether the effect is observable at all is set by the decoding rule

The eligibility columns, not the rates, carry the second finding.

| | eligible states (of 480) | truncated by the sampler |
|---|---|---|
| **Gemma** (`top_p` 0.95) | 123 | 288 |
| **Qwen** (`top_p` 0.80) | 31 | 384 |

Same task, same margins, same truths. Qwen's nucleus is narrower, so in 384 of 480 states the
misleading answer carries probability the sampler removes — and **no number of draws reaches
it**. The phenomenon §4.4 describes is not merely rarer on Qwen; in those states it is
impossible.

This is F9 restated with a known truth, and it cuts both ways for the literature: a study that
sees no misleading rollouts has not shown the model prefers truth, and a study that sees them
has not shown it prefers falsehood. **Both observations are about the decoding configuration
at least as much as about the model.**

## S3 — Preferring a falsehood is a different failure, and must not be counted as this one

At the tightest margin the models do not merely leave the falsehood reachable; they **prefer**
it. Mean mass on the answer the log settles:

| | margin 1 affirm | states where the falsehood is preferred |
|---|---|---|
| Gemma | **0.2819** [0.2038, 0.3641] | 50/60 |
| Qwen | **0.2321** [0.1507, 0.3389] | 54/60 |

Those states are correctly **ineligible** for §4.4, which needs the truthful answer favoured.
But they are not missing data — they are an accuracy result, and the analysis counts the two
reasons for ineligibility separately and never sums them:

- **falsehood preferred** — the model is wrong. §4.4's construction does not apply.
- **truncated** — the model is right and the sampler forbids the tail. §4.4 applies and the
  decoding rule prevents it.

**Reporting one "not eligible" number would say neither**, and reading the first as evidence
of deception would be F7's error in a new setting: the model cannot count to five in a single
forward pass with reasoning disabled, and that is incompetence, not concealment.

## S4 — The external selector: a falsehood no preference produced

§4.5, and the experimental form of the paper's opening coin.

| | Gemma | Qwen |
|---|---|---|
| Emitted report was false | **0.5312** [0.4875, 0.5750] | **0.5312** [0.4875, 0.5750] |
| Preference favoured truth at the same state | 411/480 | 415/480 |

An RNG outside the model chose which of two fixed reports was returned. Nothing about the
truth and nothing about any recipient reached that draw — asserted by test on the selector's
signature, not merely by construction. On half the trials the transcript contains a falsehood,
while at the same state the model's own scores favoured the truth in about 86% of cases.

**Any methodology that infers a deceptive selection mechanism from the trajectory containing a
falsehood misclassifies this selector**, which is non-deceptive by construction. That is a
statement about the methodology and needs no model behaviour to be true, which is what makes
it the stronger of the two arms.

The 14% where preference did not favour truth are the S3 states, and they are reported rather
than excluded: this arm inherits the accuracy failure and does not hide it.

## S5 — A standing answer bias explains half the table

Mean probability on `Yes`, pooled across every counting query: **0.372** (Gemma), **0.372**
(Qwen). The models lean toward `No`.

Every denying cell has `No` as the truth, so a bias that has nothing to do with the log makes
the model look truth-favouring there. Compare margin 3: affirmations sit at 0.913 and denials
at 0.993, and the gap is not competence.

**This is why the pre-registration forbids pooling polarities.** A single "truth mass" number
over both would read as accuracy and would be substantially the bias.

## Numbers that must not be quoted

- **The saturating "any draw misled" indicator.** With 200 draws it approaches 1 on every eligible
  state and measures nothing; quote the per-draw rate against its prediction (S1).
- **Eligibility-dependent rates as general rates.** 0.196 is the rate *on eligible states under the
  deployment sampler*; on the 288 / 384 truncated states the rate is 0 by construction (S2).
- **S3's false-preferring states as instances of selection misattribution.** They are accuracy
  failures by the pre-registered rule.
- **Anything pooled across polarities.** The 0.372 `Yes` bias makes one polarity look truthful for
  a reason unrelated to the log (S5).
- **Any claim about factual knowledge.** The truth here is a log in the context, not a fact about
  the world.

## Weaknesses

- The truth is a log in the context, not a fact about the world. That is what makes it exact,
  and it means nothing here bears on the models' factual knowledge.
- Counting is one task. Whether the same tail behaviour appears for other computations is
  untested; F5's scope caveat applies.
- The eligible band is narrow and sampler-dependent — which is S2, reported as a finding
  rather than worked around by raising temperature. No condition here used a non-deployment
  sampler.
- Two models, family confounded with quantisation method. Per model, no pooling.
