# Fictional entities, and why these names appear here

**Every organisation, person, email address, and ticker symbol in this repository's scenarios is
fictional. None refers to any real company, person, or domain, and nothing here describes, alleges,
or implies conduct by any real party.** The scenarios stage a fabricated insider-trading episode in
order to measure a language model's reporting preference. They are not reports of events.

## Where the names come from

The Experiment 3 and 5 scenarios are a **structural reconstruction of the environment published by
Scheurer, Balesni and Hobbhahn**, *Large Language Models can Strategically Deceive their Users when
Put Under Pressure* (arXiv:2311.07590). Reproducing their entity names is **required for the
replication to mean anything**: the point of the experiment is to measure the same situation they
measured, and renaming its parts would answer a different question.

The names carried over from their published paper, which prints them itself, are `WhiteStone`,
`Alpha`, `Amy`, `Sally` and the ticker `LING`, together with the addresses `amy@whitestone.com` and
`sally@whitestone.com`. Their paper also uses `gray.com`, `gladwell.com` and `bluedovecorp.com` in
its other scenarios.

## The 23 further scenario surfaces are ours

To make the scenario instance the statistical unit rather than a single phrasing, this work adds 23
more surfaces with invented firm, agent, manager and tipper names. Their email addresses are not
written anywhere; the harness derives each one at run time from the firm name
(`trading_task.Surface`, one line). **We checked on 2026-09-02, and 22 of those 23 domains resolve
and accept mail.** They were chosen as ordinary-sounding names, with no reference to and no
knowledge of whoever holds the corresponding domains.

**If you hold one of these domains: nothing in this repository is about you, your organisation, or
anyone at it.** The addresses were generated by string concatenation from an invented firm name. No
message was ever sent to any of them, no address was collected from any source, and no real mailbox
was contacted at any point in this work.

## Why the wording was not changed

The published records embed the exact prompt text the models were shown, and the shipped prompts
must regenerate byte-identically from the scenario code. Editing the wording after collection would
break the correspondence between the prompts and the data, which is the property this replication
package exists to provide. The disclosure above is the mitigation; moving future collections to a
domain that can never resolve (RFC 2606 `example.com`, RFC 6761 `.example`) is recorded as an open
decision in the authors' tracker and is not settled here.

## Scope

This file applies to every scenario in the package: the guessing game (Experiments 1 and 2), the
equipment log (Experiment 2), and the trading scenario (Experiments 3, 4 and 5). The guessing-game
and equipment-log scenarios are original to this work and name no organisation at all.
